📋 BMI Classification & Information

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a measure of body fat based on height and weight that applies to adult men and women aged 20 and older. Use the table below to understand your BMI category.

Classification BMI Range (kg/m²) Health Risk
Underweight Below 18.5 Increased risk of nutritional deficiency and osteoporosis
Normal weight 18.5 – 24.9 Low risk — healthy weight range
Overweight 25.0 – 29.9 Moderate risk of heart disease and diabetes
Obese (Class I) 30.0 – 34.9 High risk of heart disease, diabetes, and hypertension
Obese (Class II) 35.0 – 39.9 Very high risk — medical intervention recommended
Obese (Class III) 40.0 and above Extremely high risk — consult a healthcare provider

How BMI Is Calculated

BMI is calculated using the formula: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). For example, a person weighing 70 kg with a height of 1.75 m would have a BMI of approximately 22.9, which falls within the normal weight range.

Limitations of BMI

  • BMI does not distinguish between muscle and fat — athletes with high muscle mass may have a high BMI but low body fat.
  • It does not account for fat distribution — visceral fat around the abdomen poses greater health risks than fat stored elsewhere.
  • BMI may not be accurate for pregnant women, elderly individuals, or children.
  • It does not consider age, sex, ethnicity, or bone density differences.

Healthy Weight Management Tips

  • Maintain a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins.
  • Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week.
  • Include strength training exercises at least twice a week.
  • Stay hydrated and limit sugary drinks and processed foods.
  • Consult a healthcare professional for personalized advice.
