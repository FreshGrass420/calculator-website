🏠 Mortgage Payment Guide

A mortgage is a long-term loan used to buy a home, repaid in fixed monthly payments over a set term — typically 15 or 30 years. Each payment splits into principal (what reduces the loan) and interest (the cost of borrowing), plus optional escrow items like property tax, homeowners insurance, PMI, and HOA fees.

How Monthly Payments Are Calculated

The monthly principal-and-interest payment uses the standard amortization formula:

M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1]

  • M — monthly payment
  • P — loan amount (home price minus down payment)
  • r — monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)
  • n — total number of monthly payments (years × 12)

Worked Example

Say you buy a $300,000 home with a 20% down payment ($60,000), leaving a $240,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years. The monthly principal-and-interest payment is about $1,517, and you pay roughly $306,110 in total interest over the life of the loan.

Loan Term Monthly Payment Total Interest Total Paid
30-year fixed $1,517 $306,110 $546,110
15-year fixed $2,091 $136,320 $376,320

A 15-year term has a much higher monthly payment but saves about $169,790 in interest over the shorter term.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How is mortgage monthly payment calculated?

The monthly mortgage payment is calculated using the formula M = P * [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1], where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the total number of monthly payments.

What is the standard mortgage loan term?

The most common mortgage loan terms are 15 years and 30 years. A 30-year fixed-rate mortgage is the most popular option in the United States due to lower monthly payments.

How does down payment affect my mortgage?

A larger down payment reduces your loan amount, which lowers monthly payments and total interest paid. A down payment of at least 20% typically helps you avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI).
