💰 Compound Interest Guide

Compound interest is the interest you earn on both your original money and the interest it has already generated. Over time, this "interest on interest" makes investments grow exponentially — the single most powerful force in long-term saving.

The Compound Interest Formula

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

  • A — final amount after interest
  • P — initial principal (starting balance)
  • r — annual interest rate (decimal)
  • n — number of compounding periods per year
  • t — number of years the money is invested

This CalcBro calculator also adds recurring contributions, so it handles both your lump sum and the money you invest on a regular schedule.

Worked Example

Invest $10,000 and add $100 per month at a 7% annual return compounded monthly. After 10 years you would have about $37,406 — made up of $22,000 in total contributions and roughly $15,406 in interest earned.

How Compounding Frequency Changes Growth

For a $10,000 investment at 5% over 10 years, the compounding frequency makes a small but real difference:

The earlier you start and the more often interest compounds, the faster your balance grows.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. It allows your investment to grow exponentially over time as you earn interest on interest.

How is compound interest calculated?

The compound interest formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where A is the final amount, P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the time in years.

How do monthly contributions affect compound interest?

Monthly contributions accelerate investment growth by adding to the principal regularly. Each contribution also earns compound interest, dramatically increasing total returns over long time horizons.
